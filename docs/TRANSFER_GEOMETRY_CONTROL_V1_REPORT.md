## Scientific result boundary

Natural exposure is primary. Its mean locality contrast `Q=D−O` was GLOBAL
0.0313, BLOCK 0.0375, and DIAGONAL 0.0479. This small ordering did not remain
stable under paired seed contrasts, so the preregistered claim that designed
geometry would directly determine empirical transfer geometry was **not
established**.

Teaching was a positive-control upper bound, not the primary ecology. Teaching
`Q` was GLOBAL 0.1125, BLOCK 0.0771, and DIAGONAL 0.0344. The teaching-natural
`Q` gaps were +0.0813, +0.0396, and −0.0135, respectively. This confirms that
selected exposure can change measured transfer, but it does not validate the
desired geometry ordering.

Foreign-theta specificity was weak and heterogeneous (mean `S_theta`: GLOBAL
0.0469, BLOCK 0.0813, DIAGONAL 0.0219; ranges include both signs). It should not
be described as proof of run-specific procedural learning.

The measured operators were asymmetric. The offline effective-model analysis
found modest transient amplification (natural maxima 1.216, 1.195, 1.156 for
GLOBAL, BLOCK, DIAGONAL on the declared grid), while the frozen toy dynamics
decayed across the tested relaxation/selection grid. Neither result is an LLM
society observation.

## Interpretation

The controlled-geometry prerequisite is **PARTIAL**: the protocol, exact
verifiers, paired seeds, task manifests, and transfer matrices are technically
validated, but the natural ecology did not provide a clean, reproducible
designed-to-empirical geometry mapping. This is a useful calibration result and
an explicit warning against assuming that latent factor overlap automatically
creates learnable comparative advantage.

The strongest remaining objections are unequal effective difficulty, prompt or
answer-label shortcuts, small environment-seed count, and the fact that teaching
may reveal a more identifiable exposure stream than natural experience. The
effective operator is a model-based analysis object, not an automatically valid
LLM-society Jacobian.
